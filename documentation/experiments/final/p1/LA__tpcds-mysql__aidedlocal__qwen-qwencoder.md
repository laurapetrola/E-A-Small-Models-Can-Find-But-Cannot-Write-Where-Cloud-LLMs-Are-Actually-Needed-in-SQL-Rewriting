## THE RESULT — the engine axis now exists in BOTH arms

| | **LOCAL** writer | **CLOUD** writer |
|---|---|---|
| **PostgreSQL** | **SL1** — 102 runs · reach **3** · `mech_f` **58%** | **C1** — 108 runs · reach **13** · `mech_f` **7%** |
| **MySQL** | **LA** — 67 runs · reach **3** · `mech_f` **73%** | **C4** — 52 runs · reach **8** · `mech_f` **25%** |

**The COLUMN reading is the finding:** swapping the local writer for the cloud one moves `mech_f`
from **58 → 7%** on PostgreSQL and from **73 → 25%** on MySQL. The gain exists **on both engines**,
and it is large on both. ⇒ The WRITE-wall is not an artifact of PostgreSQL.

**The ROW reading is weaker than it looks:** the local arm's reach is **3 on both engines**. This
does *not* mean "the engine doesn't matter for the local arm" — it means that with a local writer the
reach is too low to discriminate. The engine signal shows up in `mech_f` (58 × 73%), not in the reach.

## THE DECOMPOSITION OF `mech_f` — and it is the OPPOSITE of C4's

`mechanics_failed` aggregates **execution timeout** (valid SQL that doesn't finish) and **invalid
SQL**. Decomposing LA's 49 cases:

| cause | LA (aided-**local**) | C4 (aided-**cloud**) |
|---|---|---|
| **nonexistent column** | **22** | 0 |
| other / no message | 20 | 1 |
| syntax | 4 | 0 |
| nonexistent source | 1 | 3 |
| `only_full_group_by` | 1 | 0 |
| **execution timeout** | **1** | **9 (69%)** |

**In LA, the 73% `mech_f` is real WRITING failure** — 22 of 49 are *nonexistent column*, the model
inventing a column name. Only **1** is timeout.
**In C4, 69% was timeout** — the query not finishing, not the model writing badly.

⇒ **The two `mech_f` numbers do not measure the same thing, and the decomposition is what saves the
thesis:** the local arm's WRITE-wall is **real and is about writing**; the apparent "wall" of the
cloud arm on MySQL is about **execution**. Without separating them, a reviewer could say we are
calling timeout a writing failure — and on the local cells that would be false, but on the cloud ones
it would be fair.

## THREATS TO VALIDITY

- **n=3 is COVERAGE**, and SL1 is n=5. **Do not compare reaches** (3 × 3 is a coincidence of
  values, not measured equivalence). Only the outcome rates are comparable.
- **"other / no message" are 20 of 49** — records that did not keep a readable `errors` field.
  Do not redistribute by assumption; report it as its own category.
- **Cross-engine physical design:** InnoDB indexes FKs automatically, PostgreSQL does not. This is
  a NATIVE difference between the engines, stated in the methodology. Do not re-litigate — already
  discussed twice.
- **`equivalence_failed` in 4 of 67 (6%)**: the model produces valid SQL that changes the
  semantics. The S=1 gate catches it, but it shows that part of the wall is about **understanding the
  query**, not syntax.

**Legend** — outcome: `L` land · `ng` no gain · `mf` mechanical failure · `to` timeout · `eq`/`eqs` semantic/structural non-equivalence · `un` unverifiable.
**≥X%** is a **lower bound** (the original doesn't complete at real scale → the true gain is LARGER). `prov.` = **provisional** land, verified at reduced scale.
**Never compare `≥X%` with `X%`** — they are not the same ruler.
**before → after**: wall time of the ORIGINAL and of the REWRITE on the best land (this is where the % comes from). With no land, shows the median of the original — to separate a *query heavy for the DATABASE* from a *query hard for the MODEL*. `(floor)` = the original didn't complete, the time is the budget FLOOR.
**inference**: TOTAL LLM time per run (median), with the writer's share highlighted.
**index (best)**: `(measured)` = index actually created in a transaction, timed (warm-up discarded + median of 3) and undone by ROLLBACK · `(estimated)` = planner cost.
**The `workload index` column is ALWAYS ESTIMATED**, even when the one next to it is measured: it is the average of `(baseline_cost − cost_with_index)/baseline_cost` over the OTHER queries in the record — **planner cost, not time**. `agg X% (N regr)` = the others get X% cheaper on average, N get worse. **Do not read the two columns on the same ruler** — the planner overestimates (seen: 10.4% estimated × 0.8% measured on the same index).
**prov.**: the ORIGINAL doesn't complete within budget → no measured gain (only a lower bound) AND the equivalence check falls back to reduced scale, which **refutes but does not certify**. Both things have the same cause.
