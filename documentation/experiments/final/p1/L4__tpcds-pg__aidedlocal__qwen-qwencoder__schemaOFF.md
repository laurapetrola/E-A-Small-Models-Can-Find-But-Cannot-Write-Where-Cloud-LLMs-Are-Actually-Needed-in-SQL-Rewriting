## FINDING 1 — decomposing in the local arm MAKES IT WORSE

The pair with **L0c** is clean: same model, same n, same corpus, **no schema on either side**.
Only whether there is **one** agent or **two** changes:

| | agents | reach | lands (runs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0c** (raw qwen) | **1** does everything | **7**/21 | 18/102 (18%) |
| **L4** (aided-local) | **2** (finds + writes) | **3**/21 | **3**/104 (3%) |

⇒ **Giving qwen a separate writer COSTS 4 queries of reach and 6× in land rate.**

**This is stronger than `C2 × C3`**, where decomposing produced **null** (17 = 17). Here it is not
null: it is **negative**. And it closes the reading on both ends of the scale:

| scale | monolithic | decomposed | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cloud** (flash × flash) | 17 | 17 | **null** |
| **local** (qwen × qwen-coder) | **7** | **3** | **negative** |

⇒ **Decomposing never helped when the writer is at the same level as the finder.** The gain from
decomposition, measured under the law of inverse gain (mistral 1→16 etc.), comes entirely from
**being able to swap in a better writer** — not from the act of separating the roles itself.

## FINDING 2 — the SCHEMA axis, closed at 21 queries

Pair with **SL1** — identical except for schema linking:

| | runs | reach | `mech_f` | `eq_semantic` | lands | `no_gain` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L4** schema **OFF** | 104 | **3**/21 | 55% | **36%** | **3%** | 6% |
| **SL1** schema **ON** | 106 | **3**/21 | 57% | **16%** | **11%** | 12% |

**What the schema does NOT do:** it does not change the **reach** (3 = 3) and does **not reduce
mechanical failure** (55% × 57% — a tie).

**What the schema does:** it cuts **semantic equivalence failure in half** (36% → 16%) and
**triples the land rate** (3% → 11%).

**Reading:** without schema the model writes SQL that **runs but answers a different question**.
The schema doesn't teach it to write — it teaches it to write **about the right columns**. The same
3 queries are reached in both regimes; the schema just makes it get things right more often within
them.
