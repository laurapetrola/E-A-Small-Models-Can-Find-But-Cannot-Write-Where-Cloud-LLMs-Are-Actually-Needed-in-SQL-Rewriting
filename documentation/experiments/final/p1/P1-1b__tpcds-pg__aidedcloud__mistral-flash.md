⇒ **It's the cleanest demonstration of P1's thesis:** the model **was finding the strategy the whole
time**; what was missing was writing it. No other cell separates FIND from WRITE this sharply, because
in the other models the floor was already higher and the gain remains ambiguous between "found
better" and "wrote better".

### And this is where the RIGOR of n matters most

**DO NOT write that "mistral beats qwen".** P1-1b is **n=3** and C1 is **n=5** — more runs
mechanically means more chance of ≥1 land, and comparing reach between different n is a known bias of
the project. What is compared is **outcome rate**, insensitive to n: `mech_f` 6% × 7%, `no_gain`
32% × 44%.
**The VALID and strong comparison is the INTERNAL one:** mistral alone × mistral with `flash`. Same
model, same n, same base cell — 1 → 16.

## THREATS TO VALIDITY

- **n=3 is COVERAGE.** The consistency ruler (≥3/5) does not apply; this cell reports reach.
- **31 baseline timeouts for 66 runs.** Where the original doesn't complete, the denominator is a
  **floor** and the gain is a lower bound. No table shows n without the timeouts alongside it.
- **6 `timeout` outcomes** (the run itself, not the baseline) — ~9% of attempts don't finish.
- **Measured cost: US$ 0.127/run** — almost double the rest of the campaign. Mistral generates more
  re-strategy cycles, and each one is a paid call. Relevant for replication, not for validity.
- **The cell went through a balance drop:** on 08/09 the balance broke through the guard's floor
  (US$ 0.36 against a floor of 1.00) because the guard checks **between legs** and this leg lasted
  ~25 h. It was recharged before hitting zero; had it hit zero, the 402 error would have recorded a
  uniform `mechanics_failed` that **looks like a model limit and is actually billing**. No run in this
  cell was affected.

**Legend** — outcome: `L` land · `ng` no gain · `mf` mechanical failure · `to` timeout · `eq`/`eqs` semantic/structural non-equivalence · `un` unverifiable.
**≥X%** is a **lower bound** (the original doesn't complete at real scale → the true gain is LARGER). `prov.` = **provisional** land, verified at reduced scale.
**Never compare `≥X%` with `X%`** — they are not the same ruler.
**before → after**: wall time of the ORIGINAL and of the REWRITE on the best land (this is where the % comes from). With no land, shows the median of the original — to separate a *query heavy for the DATABASE* from a *query hard for the MODEL*. `(floor)` = the original didn't complete, the time is the budget FLOOR.
**inference**: TOTAL LLM time per run (median), with the writer's share highlighted.
**index (best)**: `(measured)` = index actually created in a transaction, timed (warm-up discarded + median of 3) and undone by ROLLBACK · `(estimated)` = planner cost.
**The `workload index` column is ALWAYS ESTIMATED**, even when the one next to it is measured: it is the average of `(baseline_cost − cost_with_index)/baseline_cost` over the OTHER queries in the record — **planner cost, not time**. `agg X% (N regr)` = the others get X% cheaper on average, N get worse. **Do not read the two columns on the same ruler** — the planner overestimates (seen: 10.4% estimated × 0.8% measured on the same index).
**prov.**: the ORIGINAL doesn't complete within budget → no measured gain (only a lower bound) AND the equivalence check falls back to reduced scale, which **refutes but does not certify**. Both things have the same cause.
